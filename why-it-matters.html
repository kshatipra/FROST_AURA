Your brain first

We care about your brain.

A sudden beep or ringtone triggers your startle reflex: a jolt, a blink, a quick jump in heart rate. Your attention gets yanked away from whatever you were doing.

3×more mistakes after a phone notification, even unanswered

In one study, students who got a call or text during an attention test, without even looking at it, made about three times as many mistakes. In another, people who were interrupted worked faster but felt more stressed and frustrated.

Aura uses light, not sound. It sits in the corner of your eye, so you notice it when you're ready. Designers call this calm technology: tech that informs without demanding your attention.

Sources: Stothart et al., 2015 · Mark et al., CHI 2008 · Weiser & Brown, Calm Technology, 1995

Water

Even a little thirsty changes how you feel.

1–2%of body weight

Young men who lost that much water reported more tiredness and tension, and made more errors on attention tests. Young women in a similar study felt lower in mood, had more headaches and found tasks harder.

Aura glows blue when it's been a while since your last sip, so you drink a little and often instead of catching up at night.

Sources: Ganio et al., British Journal of Nutrition, 2011 · Armstrong et al., Journal of Nutrition, 2012 (summary)

Adequate daily water intake for adults, set by the European Food Safety Authority (total water, from drinks and food). Heat, exercise and health conditions change what you need. EFSA

Breaks

Short breaks keep your energy up.

22studies reviewed

Across 22 studies, breaks of ten minutes or less were linked to more energy and less fatigue. They didn't make people work better on every task, so think of them as looking after yourself, not a productivity hack.

Aura glows amber when you've been sitting a long time. Stand up, stretch, get a refill.

Source: Albulescu et al., PLOS ONE, 2022 (summary)

One dot for each study in the review. Micro-breaks: 10 minutes or less.

Move

Standing up does more than stretch your legs.

~25%lower glucose rise

In a lab study, adults who took a 2-minute light walk every 20 minutes had a smaller blood sugar rise after a meal than when they sat for 5 hours straight.

The same amber cue works as your reminder to get up and walk for a minute.

Source: Dunstan et al., Diabetes Care, 2012 · 19 adults aged 45–65 with overweight or obesity.

Glucose response over 5 hours after a meal (incremental area under the curve, mmol/L·h).

Focus

A timer beats willpower.

24 + 6minutes

Students who studied in fixed blocks of 24 minutes with a 6-minute break felt less tired and distracted, and more focused and motivated, than students who took breaks whenever they felt like it. They got the same work done.

Source: Biwer et al., British Journal of Educational Psychology, 2023 · 87 university students.

Two hours, Pomodoro style: focus (violet) and short breaks (amber).

Breathe

Five minutes of slow breathing.

5minutes a day

People who did 5 minutes of "cyclic sighing" a day for a month (two breaths in through the nose, one long breath out) saw their mood improve more than a group doing mindfulness meditation.

A slow green light to breathe along with.Coming before mass production

Source: Balban et al., Cell Reports Medicine, 2023 · 108 people, small exploratory trial.

Ready?

Inhale through the nose, a second short inhale on top, then a long, slow exhale through the mouth.

Good vibes

And the "water vibrations"?

You may have heard that water picks up vibrations, or changes with music and kind words. There's no reliable scientific evidence for that. The experiments behind those claims were never confirmed in careful, blinded tests.

The good feeling is real, though. It comes from the ritual: you stop, breathe, notice the light and take a sip. That moment is for you.
